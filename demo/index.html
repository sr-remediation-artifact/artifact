    Which repair to fund first

    Being close to the truth and putting the repairs in the right order are two different things. An operator with budget for one repair does not need the number to be right, only the order.

    Where it does not win

    How is the estimate made? four steps, no query to the engine

    Nothing below is specific to this vendor or to security. It needs a score observed repeatedly over time, and a way to tell from two consecutive observations which kind of work was done.

    1. Pair consecutive observationsSort each organization's records by date and pair each with the next. The difference between the two scores is what actually happened.
    2. Group findings into repairsFindings that a team would schedule together become one repair, weighted by severity. That weighted count is the work still waiting.
    3. Call a repair done when its count fallsThis is inferred from the trace rather than from a work order, and it is the main assumption. The checks in the panel above bound it.
    4. Predict the change directly, from the earlier record onlyInputs are the configuration as it stood, the work waiting in each repair, and which repair is being funded. Nothing the work produced may enter.

    A second model, trained on the first one's errors on organizations it had never seen, withholds estimates it judges unreliable rather than answering anyway.

    What can I check? protocol, populations, limits

    What this cannot tell you

    Work that was funded and produced no measurable change never enters the population, because a repair is recognised from a fall in its findings. So every number here is conditional on work that reached the platform's view. Closing that gap needs the platform's own notification logs.

    The underlying records stay closed: organizations remain identifiable from raw configuration content even with their identifiers removed.
